# Project Background

## Context

The Telepharmacy Mobile PoC was developed between **April 2021 and September 2022** as a funded applied R&D project with an industry pharmacy partner under NTUC FairPrice Co-operative Ltd.

The project explored how an existing telepharmacy workflow could be redesigned for **direct patient access from a personal mobile device** during the COVID-19 period.

At the start of the project, the concept was assessed at **Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 3**. By project completion, it had progressed to **TRL 5**.

## Problem statement

Traditional telepharmacy access could still depend on the patient being physically present at a pharmacy branch before connecting to a remote pharmacist.

The PoC explored a mobile-first alternative that would allow patients to:

- perform a preliminary assessment;
- connect with a pharmacist remotely;
- complete verification steps digitally;
- identify a nearby pharmacy location for collection;
- support subsequent medicine-order workflow steps.

The design also considered privacy and convenience for users who may prefer consulting from home rather than at a pharmacy counter.

## Target use cases

The project considered users such as:

- people who need medicine unexpectedly;
- users who already know what they need from prior experience;
- users who require pharmacy-only medicines but face difficulty visiting a physical branch.

## Scope of the prototype

The planned and completed PoC capabilities included:

1. Android and iOS mobile application
2. Preliminary-assessment chatbot
3. Notification/messaging of assessment information
4. Video consultation
5. GPS/location support for nearby pharmacy pickup
6. QR-code generation for verification
7. One-time patient registration
8. Patient and pharmacist login backed by a private/local database
9. Consultation-video storage on the host device

## Validation and status

The prototype was demonstrated to the industry pharmacy partner and validated as a relevant mobile extension of the existing telepharmacy setup.

The project remained a **prototype / proof-of-concept** rather than a commercial production deployment.
